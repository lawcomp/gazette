THE IMPORTANCE OF LEGAL EDUCATION IN NIGERIA

By James Ukeme Category: Law and Society Published: November 2, 2026

---

Introduction

Legal education plays an important role in the development of every society. It equips individuals with the knowledge of their rights, duties, responsibilities and obligations under the law.

In Nigeria, understanding the law is particularly important because the legal system governs virtually every aspect of human interaction, including business transactions, family relationships, property ownership and criminal responsibility.

However, legal education should not be restricted to law students and legal practitioners. Every citizen should have a reasonable understanding of the laws that regulate society.

1. Understanding the Nigerian Legal System

Nigeria operates a legal system influenced by several sources of law, including:

The Constitution.

Legislation enacted by the National Assembly and State Houses of Assembly.

Judicial precedents.

Customary law.

Islamic law, where applicable.

Received English law.

The Constitution remains the supreme law of the country.

Legal Authority: Section 1(1) of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended).

> The Constitution is the supreme law of the land, and any law inconsistent with its provisions is void to the extent of its inconsistency.

2. Important Judicial Decisions

Judicial decisions help explain how courts interpret and apply legal principles.

For example:

[Marbury v Madison (1803)] – This case established the principle of judicial review in the United States.

[Donoghue v Stevenson (1932)] – This landmark decision established the modern principle of negligence and the neighbour principle.

3. Why Legal Education Matters

Legal education provides several benefits:

1. It promotes respect for the rule of law.

2. It helps citizens understand their fundamental rights.

3. It encourages responsible citizenship.

4. It reduces exploitation and unlawful practices.

5. It improves access to justice.

4. Frequently Asked Questions

Question 1: What is the supreme law of Nigeria?

Answer: The Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended).

Question 2: What is judicial precedent?

Answer: Judicial precedent is a legal principle under which courts follow relevant legal decisions previously made by superior courts.

5. Conclusion

Legal education is essential for building a society where justice, fairness and accountability are respected. Citizens who understand the law are better equipped to protect their rights and fulfil their legal obligations.
